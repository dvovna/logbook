A Slack app · open source

Your team's hours and days off, kept in Slack.

People ask for leave and log their hours in the Slack they already have open. Managers approve leave from a message and hours from one Approvals inbox. Reports and billing come from the same records, so nobody copies anything into a spreadsheet.

How it works

Time off, hours and billing, step by step

The forms and messages below are the ones Logbook sends, shortened where ⋯ stands. Only the people and the client are made up.

01 · Time off

From “can I take Thursday and Friday off?” to the timesheet

Anna asks for two days of annual leave. Her manager and HR approve them from their DMs, and with one click she puts the approved days into her week as hours.

  1. Mon 8 Jun, 09:12 · Anna

    Asks for the days

  2. Mon 8 Jun, 10:40 · Tom, her manager

    Approves from a DM

  3. Mon 8 Jun, 10:41 · Anna

    Hears who decides next

  4. Mon 8 Jun, 14:05 · Anna

    Gets the answer, logs it as time

    The click logs each working day of the leave as approved time on the project an admin picked for annual leave, 8 hours a day unless the admin set another length.

02 · Hours

A week of hours, from the form to approval

Anna fills in her week in one form and sends it for approval. Her manager gets one message about it, and a teammate who forgot gets a reminder on Monday.

  1. Fri 19 Jun, 17:30 · Anna

    Fills in the week

    Left out at ⋯: Hiring's five days, and the fields to add a project and show the weekend. The approved days off are in the week already and can't be changed here.

  2. Fri 19 Jun, 17:30 · Tom

    Gets one message for the week

  3. Mon 22 Jun, 09:00 UTC · Mark

    Forgot, gets a reminder

    Mark sends his three entries from Home before Tom opens Approvals.

  4. Mon 22 Jun, 09:40 · Tom

    Approves the lot in one place

03 · Billing

From approved hours to the numbers for an invoice

At the start of the month, finance locks a client's approved hours with their rates and takes a CSV to the accounting tool. Logbook doesn't make the invoice itself.

  1. Wed 1 Jul · Olga, finance

    Opens the client's month

  2. Wed 1 Jul · Olga

    Locks it in a snapshot

  3. Wed 1 Jul · Olga

    Takes the CSV to accounting

    Sent from the snapshot's ⋯ menu. A line per entry, with the date, person, project, hours, and the rate and amount it was locked with.

  4. Fri 3 Jul · Olga

    Marks it invoiced

    The uninvoiced report lists, per client, the approved billable hours that no snapshot holds yet.

Features

Policies, rates, reminders and reports

Leave policies
A policy sets the vacation and bonus days per year and how many unused days carry over to the next year, which Logbook works out on its own. The company rules name one policy for everyone, and a team can keep its own instead. The year runs from each person's contract start date, or over the calendar year. Without a policy, Logbook records time off and checks no balance.
Holidays
Public holidays imported by country and region. Add your company's own days, or turn off the ones you work on. Working days skip weekends and holidays.
Approval levels
The team's managers first. The second level, such as HR, comes from the company rules unless the team keeps rules of its own. A level needs one approver or all of them, and when an approver is away, HR or an admin can approve for them.
Roles
Admin, HR, manager, finance and member. Managers see their teams. Only admins and finance see rates and amounts.
Reminders
Every Monday from 09:00 UTC, people with drafts or rejected time from earlier weeks get a message saying how much and from which days.
Who hasn't submitted
For a period of up to a year, the people who logged nothing or still have drafts or rejected time. Managers, HR, finance and admins can run it, and managers see their own teams.
Time reports
Hours by client, project, person or team: billable, approved, and amounts for those allowed to see them. The report's entries come to you as a CSV file in a DM.
Rates
An hourly rate per project, or a person's own rate on a project. One currency per workspace.
Uninvoiced time
Per client, the approved billable hours that are in no snapshot yet, and the snapshots not marked invoiced.
Teams from a file
The bot sends a template with everyone's team and team lead. Fill it in, send it back as CSV or Excel, check the preview and apply it; Undo restores the teams for 7 days. Admins also load past vacations from a CSV when you move over from spreadsheets.
New people
When someone joins Slack, HR gets a message asking which team they're on, and the first answer places them.

Self-hosting

Runs on your server

There is no hosted version yet. You run Logbook yourself, and the data stays in your database.

  • A Supabase project for the database. The free plan runs it, though it keeps no backups.
  • A checkout of the release with pnpm, to apply the database migrations.
  • A server with Docker or Node 26 that Slack can reach over HTTPS.
  • A Slack app created from the manifest in the repository.
  • One deployment can serve several Slack workspaces, each with its own data, once the Slack app's public distribution is on.
  • Uninstalling the app removes its token at once. The workspace's data is deleted 30 days later (you can change that), and installing again before then restores it.
